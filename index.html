RESEARCH STATEMENT

Connecting approaches that are often studied separately.

My research explores how microbial communities interact with the built water environment and how those interactions influence the persistence, transport, and control of opportunistic pathogens. I am particularly interested in understanding microbial ecology not as an isolated biological process, but as something shaped by the physical, chemical, and engineered environments through which microorganisms move and grow.

My approach brings together multiple perspectives and methods to understand these relationships. I combine environmental engineering and water-quality concepts with microbial ecology, molecular biology, and quantitative approaches to examine how microorganisms interact with one another and with water infrastructure.

A central focus of my current research is the ecology of Legionella pneumophila and other opportunistic microorganisms in potable water distribution and premise plumbing systems. I am interested in how microbial communities, biofilms, sediments, and engineered surfaces influence pathogen persistence and transport, and in how interactions between microorganisms may create opportunities for new approaches to pathogen control.

More broadly, I am interested in bringing together microbial community ecology, pathogen biology, water treatment, and environmental engineering. By connecting these perspectives, my research seeks to develop a more complete understanding of why opportunistic pathogens persist in engineered water systems and how that knowledge can be translated into practical strategies for protecting public health.
